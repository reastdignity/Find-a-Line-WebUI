## 0. Does the GDPR apply at all (private operator, no income)
- The household exemption does not cover publication to anyone on the internet | "which is clearly not the case with the processing of personal data consisting in publication on the internet so that those data are made accessible to an indefinite number of people" (C-101/01 Lindqvist, para 47) | http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/62001CJ0101 | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- GDPR recital 18: exempt only "a purely personal or household activity and thus with no connection to a professional or commercial activity" | http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32016R0679 | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Inference: the public site (accounts for strangers) is a controller under the GDPR whatever its income; the local app on a user's own PC is that user's household use, and the project is no controller of what it processes there.

## 1. GDPR when the visitor's browser loads third-party content
### 1a. Joint controllership (C-40/17 Fashion ID) and the roles Google and Esri claim
- An operator embedding content that makes the browser send data to the provider "can be considered to be a controller", but only for "the collection and disclosure by transmission of the data at issue" (operative part 2) | http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/62017CJ0040 | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- It is triggered on page load: "as soon as the visitor consults that website, regardless of whether or not the visitor ... has clicked" (para 75) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Legal basis: "it is necessary that that operator and that provider each pursue a legitimate interest" (operative part 3) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Consent "must be given prior to the collection and disclosure by transmission" and "it is for the operator of the website, rather than for the provider of the social plugin, to obtain that consent" (para 102) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- The duty to inform "is incumbent also on that operator", limited to the operations it co-determines (operative part 4) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Joint control "does not require that there be an arrangement between those entities" (C-683/21, operative part 2) | http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/62021CJ0683 | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- GDPR Art. 26(2): "The essence of the arrangement shall be made available to the data subject." Art. 26(3): rights may be exercised "against each of the controllers" | http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32016R0679 | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Google calls both parties independent controllers: "each party: (a) is an independent controller of Controller Personal Data" (Controller Terms 4.1) | https://business.safety.google/controllerterms/ | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- The EEA Maps ToS binds us to those terms: "Google and Customer agree to the then-current Google Controller-Controller Data Protection Terms" (4.5) | https://cloud.google.com/terms/maps-platform/eea (raw HTML) | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- What Google collects: "Google collects data (e.g., search terms, IP addresses, latitude and longitude coordinates)" (EEA ToS 4.4) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- We must not hand Google user data: "Customer will not provide to Google (a) any End User’s personally identifiable information; or (b) any End User’s Personal Data." (EEA ToS 4.2) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Esri is a processor: "Customer is the Controller and Esri is the Processor or Service Provider of Personal Data" (DPA G666) | https://www.esri.com/content/dam/esrisites/en-us/media/legal/gdpr-data-processing-addendums/data-process-addend.pdf | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- The Location Platform Agreement points to that DPA: "Esri will process personal data according to the terms of the Data Processing Addendum" (11.5) | https://www.esri.com/content/dam/esrisites/en-us/media/legal/platform/platform-legal.pdf (revised November 21, 2025) | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- The DPA is pre-signed and customers countersign it without returning it (Esri's pages, per the search summary) | https://trust.arcgis.com/en/compliance/data-processing-addendum.htm | accessed 2026-11-01 | UNVERIFIED
- Inference: the labels in contracts do not undo Fashion ID. For Google 3D Maps the site is a joint controller for the moment of collection and transmission (the visitor's IP address, browser data and the viewed area going to Google), and Google is the sole controller of its own later use. For Esri the site is the controller and Esri its processor, so there is no joint control but an Art. 28 contract is needed (Esri's DPA). No user id, name or run title may go into any request to Google (Map IDs, URL parameters).

### 1b. The IP address as personal data (C-582/14 Breyer)
- A dynamic IP address is personal data for a website provider "where the latter has the legal means which enable it to identify the data subject with additional data which the internet service provider has about that person" (operative part 1) | http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/62014CJ0582 | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- The Munich court takes the abstract approach: "Dabei reicht es aus, dass für die Beklagte die abstrakte Möglichkeit der Bestimmbarkeit der Personen hinter der IP-Adresse besteht." (Rn. 5) | https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/Y-300-Z-BECKRS-B-2022-N-612 | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED

### 1c. LG München I, 20.01.2022, 3 O 17493/20 (Google Fonts), official publication Bayern.Recht
- Editorial headnote 1: a violation where the operator passes the visitor's "dynamische IP-Adresse automatisiert und ohne Zustimmung des Dritten an Google weiterleitet" | https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/Y-300-Z-BECKRS-B-2022-N-612 | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- No legitimate interest, because a non-transmitting alternative existed: "denn Google Fonts kann durch die Beklagte auch genutzt werden, ohne dass beim Aufruf der Webseite eine Verbindung zu einem Google-Server hergestellt wird" (Rn. 8) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Damages: "100,00 €" (Tenor 3); the court weighed the US transfer, "wobei dort kein angemessenes Datenschutzniveau gewährleistet ist" (before the DPF, citing Schrems II) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- A later chamber (LG München I, 30.03.2023, 4 O 13063/22) rejected Google Fonts mass claims (abuse of rights, proof) | search-result summaries (dr-datenschutz.de, it-recht-kanzlei.de) | accessed 2026-11-01 | UNVERIFIED
- CJEU C-300/21: "the mere infringement of the provisions of that regulation is not sufficient to confer a right to compensation", but no threshold of "a certain degree of seriousness" (operative parts 1, 2) | http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/62021CJ0300 | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Inference, what it implies here: (i) loading Google's script with the page, before the user asks for 3D, is exactly the Fonts pattern: avoid. (ii) Google's photorealistic 3D cannot be self-hosted (raw 3D tiles are blocked for EEA projects, caching is not allowed), so the "alternative existed" argument is weaker, but a consent click ends that debate. (iii) Esri tiles may not be cached either (3.1(d)(6), item 3), and Esri is a processor, so legitimate interest is defensible, as for today's 2D satellite layer. (iv) A 3D view of our own DEM and orthophotos served from our own origin sends nothing to third parties: the strongest variant. If it pulls orthophoto tiles straight from the national WMS/WMTS, it is the same pattern as today's 2D map (EU/Swiss public bodies, USGS for US places). (v) This is a German first-instance judgment, not binding in Poland; I found no Polish judgment like it (a negative search result, UNVERIFIED).

### 1d. A click-to-load notice: consent or legitimate interest
- A click can be consent: "physical motions can be qualified as a clear affirmative action" (EDPB 05/2020, para 83) | https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- It must come first: "consent must always be obtained before the controller starts processing" (para 90); withdrawal must be possible "equally as easily" (para 114) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- A wall that blocks content until the user accepts is no free choice: "It is not presented with a genuine choice." (para 41, about "Accept cookies") | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- WP29's test for the exemption of a requested function: "the user (or subscriber) did a positive action to request a service with a clearly defined perimeter" (WP194, criterion B) | https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/opinion-recommendation/files/2012/wp194_en.pdf | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- But a third party's tracking cookies "cannot be exempted under CRITERION B"; a player's session cookies for content the user chose "matches CRITERION B" | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- The EDPB leaves the exemptions to national law and authorities: "should be analysed on a case-by-case basis accounting for the relevant member state transposition(s)" (Guidelines 2/2023, para 4) | https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- EDPB Guidelines 1/2024 (legitimate interest) found only as version 1.0 for consultation (8 Oct 2024), no final text | search of edpb.europa.eu | accessed 2026-11-01 | UNVERIFIED
- No UODO guidance found on click-to-load or embedded third-party content (searches of uodo.gov.pl) | accessed 2026-11-01 | UNVERIFIED (negative result)
- Inference, recommendation. Google 3D: a consent gate. Nothing is requested from Google before the click. The notice names Google and says what goes to Google (IP address, browser data, the area viewed), that Google uses it for its own purposes and moves it to the USA under the DPF, and links Google's Privacy Policy. It has a checkbox "remember in this browser" and a way to revoke. The 2D map and our own 3D stay usable without it (a genuine choice). Esri: no gate needed beyond the 3D button; the privacy page names Esri, the legal basis (legitimate interest) and the processor contract. Our own data: nothing new, unless orthophotos come straight from third parties.

## 2. Prawo komunikacji elektronicznej, art. 399 (storage and access on the device)
- Allowed only if the user "zostanie uprzednio poinformowany w sposób jednoznaczny, łatwy i zrozumiały" (purpose; how to set conditions) and then "wyrazi na to zgodę" (ust. 1 pkt 1-2) | https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/DocDetails.xsp?id=WDU20240001221 (text read: https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2024/1221/text.html) | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- The exemption: no consent where storage or access "jest konieczne do: ... 2) dostarczania usługi telekomunikacyjnej lub usługi świadczonej drogą elektroniczną, żądanej przez abonenta lub użytkownika końcowego" (ust. 3) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Consent through the browser: the user "może wyrazić zgodę ... za pomocą ustawień oprogramowania" (ust. 2) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- But under EU law a cookie consent is "not validly constituted" by a pre-checked box, and the rule applies "whether or not the information stored or accessed ... is personal data" (C-673/17 Planet49, operative parts 1, 2) | http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/62017CJ0673 | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- A Polish extra: providers "mogą instalować oprogramowanie w telekomunikacyjnym urządzeniu końcowym" only after information and consent "na jego instalację i używanie" (ust. 4) | PKE (URL above) | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Inference on ust. 4: a script run in the browser is not usually treated as "installed"; a Service Worker or an installable PWA might be. Unsettled.
- Enforcement: a fine for anyone who "przechowuje informacje w urządzeniach końcowych abonenta lub użytkownika końcowego ... niezgodnie z art. 399" (art. 444 ust. 1 pkt 82), imposed by "Prezes UKE" (art. 446 ust. 1). Without revenue (or up to 500 000 zł): "karę pieniężną w wysokości nieprzekraczającej 15 000 zł" (art. 446 ust. 3) | PKE (URL above) | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- None of the five amending acts (Dz.U. 2025 poz. 637, 820; 2026 poz. 252, 815, 1296) contains "art. 399" (a text search of their PDFs from api.sejm.gov.pl) | https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2024/1221 (references) | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Local storage is in scope once data leaves the device: "when this information or any derivation of this information is accessed, Article 5(3) ePD would apply" (EDPB 2/2023, para 44) | EDPB 2/2023 (URL above) | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Even a cache counts as storage: delivery "does constitute storage, at the very least through the caching mechanism of the client-side software" (para 50, about tracking pixels and links) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Scripts that make the browser send data are in scope: "JavaScript code, where the accessing entity instructs the browser of the user to send asynchronous requests" (para 33) | same | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Google makes it our job: "Customer’s use of the Services in the Customer Application will comply with applicable privacy laws, including laws regarding Services that store and access Cookies on End Users’ devices." (EEA ToS 4.1) | https://cloud.google.com/terms/maps-platform/eea | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Google's EU user consent policy (incorporated by EEA ToS 4.1 "if applicable"): "You must obtain end users’ legally valid consent to: the use of cookies or other local storage where legally required" | https://www.google.com/about/company/user-consent-policy/ | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- A remembered choice: WP29 exempts only short-lived preference cookies; "only session (or short term) cookies storing such information are exempted", and a visible "uses cookies" note next to the control makes a longer one consented (WP194, para 103) | WP194 (URL above) | accessed 2026-11-01 | VERIFIED
- Inference, when a third-party script needs consent: (1) whatever Google's 3D script stores or reads for Google's own purposes (analytics, product improvement, ads) needs prior consent under art. 399; (2) storage strictly needed to show the 3D view the user asked for (session state, tile cache) falls under ust. 3 pkt 2, but who judges a third party's script "konieczne" is unsettled; (3) our own "remember my choice" flag in localStorage needs a visible label at the checkbox; (4) Esri tile requests that set no cookies need no consent (whether they set any, and whether an Esri SDK writes local storage: R2/R3). Whether Google's `Map3DElement` writes cookies or local storage is R1 item 3. The consent gate of 1d covers art. 399 and the GDPR in one click.
