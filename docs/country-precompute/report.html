Find-a-Line · feasibility report · 2 November 2026

Highline counts for whole countries

Can Poland, Switzerland and Utah be searched offline in fixed tiles, keeping only how many lines and spots each square kilometre holds? 44 measured searches, the relief of the three countries and both machines' benchmarks say: yes, after four pieces of work, in days of computing and up to two weeks of downloading.

Branch country-precompute · main 221dd10 · rough figures (about ±50 %; ranges from a bootstrap over the sampled places) · the same report as docs/country-precompute.md

The answer

A country can be computed offline on either machine, but not with today's search as it is. The download decides the calendar in Poland; the long lines in high mountains decide the computing and, above all, the memory.

  • Slovakia, the first step (the user's choice of 2026-11-02): our own 2 km tiles of ÚGKK SR's 1 m terrain and surface models are on this PC (133 GB), so nothing is downloaded and the pilot tests the counting, the spots, the driver and the layer on a whole country at once; computing S, M and L (30-5000 m) takes about 1.8 h on the 9900X and 4.9 h on the 5650G (30-2000 m: 1.6 h and 4.3 h), and the cache holds 0.14 TB, a copy of the mirror.
  • Poland: the download takes two weeks for terrain and surface models at the 5.9 MB/s that geoportal.gov.pl gave us (one week for the terrain model alone); computing then takes hours, because nine tenths of Poland are flat and the grids, not the lines, take the time.
  • Switzerland: 1.7 days to download at swisstopo's request rate; the M band (300-2000 m) at 2 m takes 3.6 days on the 9900X (1.6-5.0 by the bootstrap) and 9 days on the 5650G, because the Alps hold about 190 billion such lines (10 to 23 million per km² in the steepest strata).
  • Utah: a day to download, a day to compute S and M at 1 and 2 m on the 9900X (2.7 days on the 5650G); the canyons of the Colorado Plateau are as dense as the Alps.
  • The L band (2-5 km): at 5 m it adds 3-27 % to a country's time (13 min for Slovakia, 1.3 h for Poland, 1.8 h for Utah, 3.7 h for Switzerland on the 9900X, about 2.5 times as long on the 5650G), so 30-5000 m costs about what 30-2000 m does. Its lines are as combinatorial as the M band's: up to 0.6 million per km² at 5 m, 102 million in Lauterbrunnen's valley (293 km²). Canyonlands at 5 m is scaled from 10 m for now (section 7).
  • What has to be built first (section 6.5): a counting mode of the line search (each evaluated part of lines goes into the counts and is dropped: today a tile of the Alps' steepest terrain at 2 m would hold 260 GB of lines), a spot that can be counted on the fly, a country driver (a fixed tile grid, K tiles at once, resume), and pretension by length in the search itself.
  • Resolution (section 1): S at 1 m, M at 2 m, L at 5 m. A 2 m grid keeps the ranking of the S band's cells (Spearman 0.96-0.98) with half its lines; 5 m is a different search for the M band (4 % of the lines, 15 % of the cells with a spot lost) and needs anchors from 45° instead of 60°.
  • The counts themselves: in big-wall terrain every anchor on one wall pairs with thousands on the other, so a raw line count measures the walls more than the highlines. The layer needs a coarser spot (anchor clusters) and its filters (prominence, height, wall angle) as floors.
  • Legality (section 5): allowed for all three; GUGiK's NMT and NMPT need no request, swisstopo asks for contact before a load above its limits (36 a minute stays below them; the e-mail is in local/mail-drafts.md), USGS asks for a description of the processing. Filling gaps from FABDEM binds the project to non-commercial use.

The idea and what was decided

A country is searched offline in tiles of a fixed size. A tile does not keep its lines: it keeps the number of lines and of spots per cell (for example 1 km), split into the bins of the filters, and the cells are summed into larger ones (2, 4, 8 km...) without computing anything again. The app shows the cells as a map layer that can be filtered. Line positions and profiles are not stored; a user who wants the lines of a cell runs an ordinary search there.

  • The S band at 2 m keeps 44-76 % of the lines and the ranking of the cells (ρ 0.96-0.98); it has more spots, because fewer lines break today's greedy groups into more pieces. The shortest lines suffer most: 30-60 m keep 43-81 % at 2 m and 8-23 % at 5 m.
  • The S band at 5 m keeps 11-30 % of the lines; the cells still rank alike (ρ 0.87-0.96), but lines of 30-60 m are mostly gone, as README warns for Spain's 5 m data.
  • The M band at 5 m keeps 4 % of the lines of 2 m and 27 % of the spots, and 15 % of the cells with a spot at 2 m have none at 5 m: at 5 m a 60° anchor needs a wall of about 17 m or more (the Horn gradient spans 10 m), so many rims of 2 m disappear. 5 m is a different search, not a thinner one.
  • The M band at 1 m was not measured: at 2 m the Lauterbrunnen sample produced 363 million lines in 50 km² (94 minutes of grouping on one core; its run was lost while saving) and Zion ran out of memory. 1 m holds four times the pixels and, judging by the S band, about twice the lines; it is out of reach until lines are counted without being kept.
  • Which grid is right? None is the truth: finer grids see narrower rims and more of the clearance's detail, coarser ones smooth both. For a count layer the cells must rank alike, and the counts must be labelled with the grid they come from.

Anchors are pixels of at least 60° (the user's choice for 1 and 2 m). On 2 m grids 45° adds little; on 5 m grids it adds a lot:

At 5 m the Horn gradient averages a wall over 10 m, so a 60° pixel needs a wall of about 17 m; with 45° the 5 m grid finds 29 % of the lines of the 2 m grid at Lauterbrunnen instead of 6 %. For bands on 5 m grids the threshold should be 45° (50° is queued, not measured yet).

1.4 Recommendation

  • S (30-300 m) at 1 m. The data is 1 m anyway (Poland, Utah), or the 0.5 m files cost no more requests than the 2 m ones (Switzerland: the same 41,445 files, request-limited); the computing is hours. 2 m is the fallback where 1 m data is missing (ρ ≥ 0.96).
  • M (300-2000 m) at 2 m, with the counting mode. Without it, 5 m with 45° anchors and small tiles is the only M band today's code can hold, and it is a coarser search.
  • L (2000-5000 m) at 5 m (10 m keeps 7 % of its lines and a third of its spots, though the cells rank alike (ρ 0.97); 5 m costs minutes to hours a country, so there is no reason to go coarser).
  • That is the strategy "split 1/2/5 m" of the tables below; "split 2/5/10 m" is what runs in hours but sees much less.

  • Downloading and computing overlap: a country driver computes the tiles whose data is in while the next ones download, so a run takes about the longer of the two, not their sum.

4.2 What a country run computes

Per tile: step 1 (stitching the layers, filling water and gaps), step 2 (anchors, prominence and drop), step 3 (the pair search) and the counts; not the coordinates, profiles, map files or lines.csv. Steps 1-2 run on one core, step 3 on all of them. The times assume:

  • lines counted, not kept: today's search keeps every line until it is saved; in the densest M stratum at 2 m (10-23 million lines per km²) even a tile of 2 km would hold 40 GB of lines, a tile of 5 km 260 GB;
  • spots not grouped as today: today's grouping is single-threaded and grows with the lines; it would add 18 h to Switzerland's M band on the 9900X if the lines fitted at all;
  • K tiles at once: as many as the memory allows at 100 B per pixel of the grids, at most one per physical core, each process keeping 85 % of a core; the fastest tile core of 2-60 km is chosen.

4.3 Per country

The numbers of each country, variant and grid strategy are in the overview at the top (choose the country, the variant and the DSM there); the tables of all of them are in the report's Markdown.

  • Poland is grid-bound: 13-25 core-hours of steps 1-2 per band against an hour of pair search; twelve tiles at once make it hours. Without the NMPT 8-9 % less.
  • Switzerland's M band at 2 m is the pair search in the Alps: 85 h on the 9900X (39-119 h), 9 days on the 5650G. The same band at 5 m takes 2 h but sees a few per cent of the lines (section 1).
  • Utah's S and M bands at 1 and 2 m: 24 h (14-31 h) on the 9900X, 22 h of it the M band's pair search; the canyon country (Zion's cells, scaled from 5 m: section 7) is as dense as the Alps' walls.
  • The L band at 5 m takes 13 min (Slovakia) to 3.7 h (Switzerland) on the 9900X; its pair search costs 40 µs a line, the most of the three bands, because a 2-5 km line has the most pixels to check.

Two duties need work when the layer is built: a methods page (USGS asks for a description of the modifications) and a combined credit block (a draft is in notes/legal.md, section 4).

6. Further points

6.1 Tiles: a fixed grid, their size, how many at once

  • A fixed tile grid per country, in the source's CRS (EPSG:2180, LV95, UTM 12N), with cores of whole kilometres: the 1 km cells of the layer never straddle two tiles, a later run (new data, a new band) produces the same tiles, and a tile's result can be replaced alone. A line belongs to the tile whose core holds its first anchor (the rule search.search_lines applies already), so no line is counted twice; a spot whose lines start in two tiles is counted in both (6.2).
  • The overlap is the longest line + 300 m (prominence span) + 2 pixels around the core: 0.6 km for S, 2.3 km for M, 5.3 km for L. A tile's grid work grows with ((core + 2 × overlap) / core)², so small tiles waste time in their overlaps (an L tile of 10 km computes 4.3 times its core) and large ones need memory (100 B per pixel of the grids: an M tile of 10 km at 2 m holds 5.3 GB).
  • Tiles at once: steps 1-2 run on one core, the pair search on all of them, so a country run runs K tiles at once, each with its own pair-search threads. The web worker's slots do this for jobs; a country driver needs it for tiles (roadmap: "Tiles of a large area in parallel processes", deferred for the public site, where the slots are full anyway; offline it pays).

6.2 The result: a sparse counter cube, and what a spot is

  • Lines are counted exactly: each line adds 1 to its key (cell, bin of length, bin of each filter). A filter of the layer sums the keys whose bins pass, a coarser cell sums cells: nothing is computed again.
  • Spots need care. Today a spot is a group of lines (greedy grouping by the distance of both anchors, mapping.group_lines), made after all lines of a tile are found, on one core, with all lines in memory. For a country layer:
    1. It does not scale to the M band in big-wall terrain: Lauterbrunnen at 2 m has 7 million lines per km² (Zion probably more), and grouping its 50 km² took 94 minutes. A country run must count lines without keeping them, so a spot must be something that can be counted on the fly.
    2. Today's spot is fine-grained there: the M band at 2 m has 10,000-40,000 spots per km² in the steepest strata (one per 25-100 m²), because the grouping distance is 20 m. As a count that tells about the walls, not about the places a person would rig.
    3. A filter changes the spots: in the app a filter acts before grouping. A cube can keep, per spot, the best value of each filter among its lines (a spot passes when any of its lines could): exact for one filter, an upper bound when several are combined, because the best clearance and the best wall angle may come from different lines. Exact counts need the spot's Pareto front of lines (more keys), or fewer filters.
  • A spot that can be counted on the fly: cluster the *anchors* first (after step 2, cheap; 50-100 m, a parameter of the run), and call a spot a pair of anchor clusters with at least one valid line. The pair search then marks a pair of clusters instead of storing lines (a hash set of 8-byte keys per tile: millions of entries, not billions of lines). It is not today's group, so the layer's spot counts will not equal the app's; the layer should say so.
  • The size of the cube (measured with cube on eight samples, 1 km cells, 12 bytes per key):

| Keys per km² | Length only | Length, prominence, wall angle, clearance | All seven filters | | --- | --- | --- | --- | | Lines | 0.6-5.3 | 2-85 | 3-208 | | Spots | 0.5-4.8 | 1-64 | 1.4-112 |

At most 2.5 KB per km² for the lines with all seven filters, so a band of Switzerland takes some tens of MB and a country with three bands and nine rigs (6.3) a few hundred MB at worst; flat country takes nearly nothing. Coarser cells (2, 4, 8 km) are sums, a few per cent more.

6.5 What the current code lacks for a country run

  1. A country driver: the fixed tile grid, K tile processes, a result file per tile (the cube's keys), resume after a stop, the inputs of each tile recorded (6.6). The web worker's job queue has the wrong shape (one search per job, its results saved as a run).
  2. A counting mode of linesearch.find_valid_lines: it evaluates the pairs it found in parts already (evaluation_parts, at least 10,000 a part) and then joins the parts into one LineTable (join_tables). A sink that bins each part into the cube and drops it removes the 450 B per line; evaluating each chunk of pairs as soon as it is found (they are held until then, 16 B a pair: up to 9 GB in a 5 km tile of the densest stratum) bounds a tile by its grids. Counts need no order of the lines, which today's results keep. With the spots of 6.2 (a cluster pair per line, a set per tile). Effort: medium, not a new kernel.
  3. Pretension by length (T(L)): only in the measuring tool, which swaps sag.EnvelopeTable for one with a rig per span. linesearch reads only the table's minimum and the rig's fall zone (line 723), so the change is small, and the results equal the plain search at a constant pretension (tests/test_perf_country.py).
  4. Several rigs in one check (6.3).
  5. One step 1 for several bands: the S, M and L bands read the same files; today each band would stitch them again, and a coarse grid costs more per pixel than a fine one (4.4).
  6. Coarse data for coarse grids: 3DEP's overviews (2, 4, 8 m: 5-44 times fewer bytes) and GUGiK's 5 m grid (photogrammetric, "Siatka 5m", about 25 times less data than the 1 m sheets; its quality on cliffs not checked).
  7. An obstacle layer instead of the DSM (roadmap: "Obstacles lowered by averaging the DSM"): DSM − DTM at the data's resolution, its maximum per grid pixel, added to the DTM: 0.66 TB of Swiss DSM files become about 6 GB.
  8. Gap filling without FABDEM for a public layer (non-commercial terms, section 5): Copernicus only.

6.6 Updates and the layer's licence

  • The data changes: GUGiK publishes new surveys per sheet, swisstopo updates its tiles survey by survey, USGS adds 3DEP projects. cache update finds new surveys of cached tiles already. A tile of the layer records its input files (path and survey date); after an update, only the tiles whose inputs changed are computed again, and their keys replace the old ones.
  • The layer is our own work (statistics, not the providers' data); its page carries the credits of section 5 and a methods page. Its own licence is the user's choice.

7.2 From samples to a country

  1. The country's relief (relief): Copernicus DEM GLO-30 over the country's OpenStreetMap outline, in blocks of about 1 km: the share of pixels of 45° or more and the relief (highest minus lowest point), over windows of 1, 3 and 5 blocks (the reach of the S, M and L lines). Of nine candidate proxies, the steep share correlates best with the samples' lines per 1 km cell (Spearman 0.77-0.84 for S, 0.80 for M at 2 m); where hardly a pixel is steep (below 0.5 %: 99.9 % of Poland), the relief splits the strata (the Jura's limestone rocks are invisible at 30 m, its valleys are not).
  2. The samples' cells: each sample's 1 km cells with their area inside the search area, the share of their lines' partners that lie inside it (Monte Carlo over the cell; cells below 0.5, for L 0.3, are left out, the others corrected by it), their lines and spots, their stratum. Lauterbrunnen's M band at 2 m takes its 5 m cells scaled to its own totals; Zion's M band at 2 m takes its 5 m cells times the median ratio of the places measured at both (26.5 for lines, 3.68 for spots: Tatras, Lauterbrunnen, Wasatch); Canyonlands' L band at 5 m takes its 10 m cells times Lauterbrunnen's ratio (14.8 for lines, 3.09 for spots), until it is measured.
  3. Counts: per stratum the lines (spots) per effective km² of all cells in it, times the country's km² in it; the water inside the outline (the Baltic's territorial sea, the Great Salt Lake) comes off the flattest stratum. A stratum without cells takes its neighbour's. The range is the 10th and 90th percentile of a bootstrap over the places (400 draws of the places with replacement; a band's range is taken alone, so a variant's range of several bands is a little wide); "mid" is the plain estimate.
  4. Other resolutions: a band's counts at another grid are scaled by the median ratio of the places measured at both (S at 2 and 5 m from S at 1 m, M at 5 m from M at 2 m).
  5. Time: section 4.4's models; a tile of core C and overlap V computes (C + 2V)² of grid; K tiles at once; the pair search keeps the measured threads busy; grids and pairs overlap but for a quarter of the shorter; the fastest core of 2-60 km wins.
  6. Download: volume / min(service, link) or files / requests per minute, whichever is longer, plus the index (section 3).

7.3 What the numbers can and cannot say

  • The times are measured on these machines and scale with the tiles; the main uncertainty is the M band's lines in big-wall terrain, where the pair search is the cost. Where the bootstrap's range is narrow (Poland) the times hold to about ±50 %; where it is wide (Switzerland, Utah), its range is the honest answer and the true spread is larger.
  • The counts depend on where the samples are: eight places stand for three countries. They were chosen where highlines are (Tatras, Jura, Lauterbrunnen, Zion, Wasatch) and where they are not (lowland, plateau, desert). The strata correct for how much of a country is steep, not for the rock: a steep slope of Polish flysch is not Jura limestone, and the Tatras' steepest cells take the density of all steep cells (mostly Lauterbrunnen's and Zion's). The bootstrap shows how much the places alone move the totals.
  • Spots are today's groups; a country run would count another kind of spot (6.2), so the spot counts here are an order of magnitude, not a forecast.
  • Not measured: the M band at 1 m and the L band at 2 m (out of reach today), the 5650G on real searches (its bench only), other pretensions than T(L).

7.4 How to update the estimate

After a change of the search, a new machine or more samples: run the searches of country-precompute/samples.sh again (one at a time under the exclusive test lock), then relief (the Copernicus tiles stay cached) and estimate, and rebuild this report and its page from the estimate: country-precompute/report/README.md.
