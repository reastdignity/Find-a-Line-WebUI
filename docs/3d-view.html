3D view of a line

Can Find-a-Line show a selected highline over a Google-Earth-like 3D landscape, with the webbing sagging under a walker and anchors you can drag? Yes, but not with Google's 3D tiles, which are closed to European projects. This page compares what is left: services, licences, cost and legality.

Recommendation

  1. A first. A KML of the line (sag curve at absolute heights, anchors, the walker's spot, credits) and links to Google Earth, swisstopo's 3D viewer and Google Maps' satellite view. The Google look of the mock today, free, no key, no consent gate. No editing, no animated walker.
  2. B with C as an add-on as the view inside the app. It meets every requirement and keeps the line on the heights the search used. With three.js it runs under today's CSP; CesiumJS only if the far field is a must.
  3. D only later, if Google's mesh covers typical spots, the site has a terms page and a consent gate, and Google answers the high-risk question. It needs its own renderer.

Decisions for you

  • The variants and their order.
  • Must the view show the far field? Yes: CesiumJS; no: three.js (below).
  • The patch's orthophoto: filled into our cache by the server, or loaded by the browser.
  • The mesh: built in the browser or on the server.
  • A terms page for the site and its wording (needed by D, firmer for C).
  • The privacy sentences proposed below (not applied).
  • Questions to Google, Esri, Fathom and GUGiK (below).
  • The local app: a provider's 3D only with the user's own key, or our data only.
  • The phone level to aim for, and a test iPhone of the 11/SE class.

The four variants

A · KML and Google Earth

  • Built in the browser from the line: the webbing as a line at absolute heights, both anchors, the walker's spot, the credits. Links: Google Earth Web at the line, swisstopo's 3D viewer for Swiss lines, Google Maps' satellite view on phones.
  • Earth Web opens a local KML "even if you're not signed in": lines, placemarks, simple tours; no 3D models. Earth Pro has models and tours but "will no longer be available for download beginning June 25, 2027".
  • The file holds only our data; a local KML "is not uploaded or submitted to Google". The camera link and swisstopo's 3D parameters are undocumented.

B · Our own data

  • The search's own DEM (1 m; 0.5 m data in Switzerland) as the ground, our orthophoto draped on it: the line sits on the heights the search used, no datum conversion.
  • Nadir orthophotos stretch over vertical walls: cliffs look smeared where the mock shows rock.
  • Textures from our cached tiles and the national services: all 20 probed services send the CORS header WebGL needs (Saxony-Anhalt only to https origins).
  • Public site and local app alike; no key; nothing new to third parties.

C · Esri

  • Terrain 3D is public and unmetered: Esri bills tiles only from its basemap hosts, and its own SDK sends no key to the terrain host. Tiles end at level 16, about 1.5 m on the ground at 50° N, built from the same national lidar; heights orthometric in the national datums.
  • World Imagery at zoom 19–21 is the national orthophoto (GUGiK, swisstopo, GKÚ, IGN); Vantor in Malta, South Tyrol and the USA.
  • 2,000,000 basemap tiles a month free, shared with the 2D satellite layer: at the limit both go dark until the next billing cycle.

D · Google 3D Maps

  • Google's mesh where it has "3D surface data", satellite on terrain elsewhere; GA in practice (default channel since 23 Oct 2025).
  • No drag on the ground: an anchor moves by a click on its new spot; heights still from our DEM ("terrain data" is Google content).
  • "Immersive Maps" per load: 5,000 free a month, then $7.00 per 1,000; a per-day quota (~150) is the hard stop; a card on a Google Cloud billing account.
  • CSP with a nonce on every script and style, 'unsafe-eval' and worker-src blob:.

Editing and the walker

  • The webbing comes from sag.ts: estimate(span, dh, rigging) for the envelope, athlete(x) for the loaded webbing with the walker at x (61 points, warm-started). Each point maps to 3D between the anchors.
  • The walker starts where the sag is largest, follows a drag along the line and walks from anchor to anchor on request; a 1.7 m low-poly mannequin.
  • Dragging an anchor takes the height from our DEM patch, never from a provider's mesh; length and sag update on every move.
  • On release the server's profile (/api/profile, as Measure) gives clearance and trees. Nothing is saved.
  • The DEM patch is a new quick-lane request, built like profile_of_line; sent as binary or base64url (standard base64 would be damaged by the public site's path redaction).

Heights

Our anchor heights are bare ground in each source's own vertical datum. What each variant needs to put the line where it belongs:

VariantIts heightsOur line needs
B, our dataoursnothing
C, Esri Terrain 3Dorthometric, from the same national DEMsnothing in PL, CH, SK, ES (to confirm); Malta differs (24 m, EGM2008)
D, Google 3D MapsABSOLUTE: "mean sea level, calculated using EGM96"our height + national geoid − EGM96 geoid at the line
A, KML"above sea level", the EGM96 geoidas D

The geoid is 22–58 m in our European countries (+39.98 m at Żabi Koń, +51.82 m at Lauterbrunnen), so a line on an ellipsoidal surface without a grid would hang tens of metres off. Taking our heights as EGM96 heights, as A and D would without the national grid, errs by up to about 1 m in Poland and 3 m in the Alps; the national grids are 0.3–1.5 MB each. Without its grid, pyproj silently returns a geoid of 0, so the code must use an explicit vgridshift pipeline that fails loudly.

Datum aside, Google's mesh is a surface model with trees and buildings, our anchors are bare ground: a line on our heights can pass through Google's trees. Google's EEA terms make us "responsible for any misalignment".

three.js fits B best: it runs under today's CSP unchanged (re-checked: 0 violations, 0 errors), is small, and every part of the scene worked. It has no globe unless 3DTilesRendererJS is added, and the camera controls and credits are ours.

CesiumJS fits if the view must show the far field like Google Earth: globe, Esri imagery and terrain built in. It costs about 1.3 MB of code, worker and asset folders outside the committed bundle, a relaxed CSP on its page, monthly API churn, and reports of lost WebGL contexts on iPhone 11, XR and SE under iOS 18.

Size, performance and phones

  • Per open (a 1.5 km square at 1 m, measured on our caches): the DEM 0.9 MB (PL) to 1.4 MB (CH) as terrain-RGB, orthophoto tiles 7 KB (PL) and 16–18 KB (CH) each. The recommended mix is about 2.2–4.2 MB; 1000 opens a month are about 3 GB from the VPS.
  • Orthophoto: zoom 17–21 is cached only 30 m around the chosen lines today, so an open needs about 120–180 more tiles, through our cache or straight from the services.
  • Mesh: 1 km of our DEM at 0.25 m error is 88k vertices in Poland, 293k in the Alps.
  • Tests: headless Edge on Windows already has WebGL2; Linux CI needs SwiftShader.

Privacy and terms, for approval

Nothing applied: the privacy page is the user's statement. Today it says maps "load background tiles (OpenStreetMap, OpenTopoMap, Esri, orthophoto services) directly: those services see your IP address." A and B need no change.

Proposal · C, Esri
Maps in your browser, the 3D view of a line included, load background tiles (OpenStreetMap, OpenTopoMap, Esri, orthophoto services) and Esri's elevation tiles directly: those services see your IP address.

And the About page's Esri sentence: "…may only be viewed in this map and in the 3D view of a line: …".

Proposal · D, Google
The 3D view of a line can show Google's 3D map. It loads only after you choose it in that view: your browser then contacts Google, which receives your IP address, browser data and the place you look at, uses them under its own Privacy Policy and may transfer them to the USA (EU-US Data Privacy Framework). The choice is remembered in this browser until you withdraw it in the view.

In the view, before anything loads: "Google 3D map: loading it sends your IP address and this place to Google… [Show Google's 3D map] [Use the site's own 3D view] [ ] Remember in this browser."

A terms page (for D; the firmer footing for C): what the site is and that its results and the 3D view are previews to check on the spot; Google's notice with links to its end-user terms and Privacy Policy; Esri's terms and "view only"; the data licences and the Copernicus liability sentence; accounts and quotas; no warranty as far as Polish law allows; contact; accepted at sign-in.

Legal

  • Roles. The public site is a controller even without income (Lindqvist). Embedding Google's map makes it a joint controller of the transmission to Google (Fashion ID); consent must come first and is the site's to collect. Esri is a processor under its DPA. The local app is the user's own household use.
  • The Munich Google Fonts judgment (3 O 17493/20, €100): passing the IP to Google without consent when an alternative existed. German, first instance; no Polish ruling or UODO guidance found.
  • Transfers. Google LLC and Esri are "Active" on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework list.
  • Device storage (Prawo komunikacji elektronicznej art. 399): consent unless "konieczne" for the requested service; the consent gate of D covers it; fines up to 15 000 zł without revenue.
  • High-risk clauses. Google bans "High Risk Activities": D shows no safety number from Google content, labels itself a preview, and the question goes to Google. Esri only disclaims: a disclaimer fits.
  • Data. PZGiK's NMT and orthophotos are free with the source stated; NMPT is not on that list (ask GUGiK). FABDEM (BY-NC-SA) in 3D is shared adapted material: non-commercial, share-alike, never exported with Esri imagery.
  • Donations change nothing for Google, Esri, PZGiK, swisstopo or Copernicus; FABDEM is a grey zone (ask Fathom); they may matter for the regulamin duty, the DSA and consumer law.

Checks and questions

By hand, before deciding on D

Is it a textured mesh or imagery on terrain? Google's coverage map, or Google Earth Web at:

  • R1 Żabi Koń
  • R2 Dolina Bolechowicka
  • R13 Kasprowy Wierch
  • R19 Lauterbrunnen · swisstopo 3D
  • R6 Dingli
  • R23 Moab

The Earth and swisstopo link syntaxes are undocumented. ArcGIS Scene Viewer, signed in with the Location Platform account, counts against its free tiles.

To the providers

  • Google: is a highline "visual preview" with our own numbers a High Risk Activity?
  • Esri: is Terrain 3D used from CesiumJS or three.js covered? May the browser read its heights while an anchor is dragged?
  • Fathom: do donations make our FABDEM use commercial?
  • GUGiK: the legal basis for reusing NMPT free of charge.

Side findings for main

  • The bundles shipped without the licence notices of their libraries: fixed on main with generate-license-file.
  • Standard base64 in a quick-lane answer would be damaged by the public site's path redaction.
  • NMPT's free reuse lacks a stated legal basis.
