# Data sources, the cache, the mosaic, the filling

What each module holds: docs/code-map/sources.md. Related rules: `providers.md` (one provider's quirks, credits,
adding a source; it loads with `sources/catalogue/`), `osm.md`, `coverage-map.md` (a source's `coverage`: after
changing it, `tools/coverage_data.py`), `search-pipeline.md` (step 1).

## Must stay in sync

- README's "Data sources" table and the ids in its `--source` row are generated by `tools/source_docs.py` from the
  catalogue (`Source.listing`, `countries`, `default_resolution`, `fallbacks`; `test_docs_list_every_source`): change
  a source, then run it. `choose.OFFLINE_BOXES` is the sources' `offline` boxes in the order of SOURCES (the first
  holding a point wins: Poland before Slovakia); a source with `regions` (ISO 3166-2, lower case) wins over its
  country's sources in its regions whatever the order of SOURCES, and its offline boxes are region boxes;
  `Source.sample` the point of the probe and `tools/check_source.py` (`test_listing_offline_and_sample`). The keys of
  `settings.ServiceKeys`, the worker's `secrets` in compose.yaml, `deploy/secrets.example/` (every Docker secret of
  both compose projects has its empty file there, from which `deploy/vps.sh` `secret_files` makes the missing files in
  `setup` and `update`, Grafana's (`GRAFANA_SECRETS`) for uid 472, the others for 10001:
  `tests/test_deploy.py::test_secret_files_listed`), secrets/README.md, docs/deployment.md section 8 and the CI's
  example secrets go together (`tests/test_keys.py::test_keys_are_the_workers_secrets`); a layer naming `keys` is
  unavailable without them. `Source.neighbours` is declared both ways and names sources whose `offline`/`bbox` boxes
  touch (`tests/test_neighbours.py::test_declared_neighbours`); a neighbour applies only where its rough boxes
  (`Source.rough_boxes`: those and its `border_boxes`) hold a border gap, so they must hold its land along every
  declared border: the offline boxes keep off a neighbour's land where they can (the first box holding a point wins)
  and so stop short of the border (Czechia's held 20-42 % of its border strips: the Saxon grid got 30 m data at
  Hřensko), and `border_boxes` hold the rest, for the neighbour fill only (they may hold a neighbour's land and never
  choose a source). A new source with neighbours, a new neighbour or changed offline boxes:
  `tools/border_study.py boxes`, every declared direction at 97 % or more
  (`tests/test_border_study.py::test_neighbour_boxes_along_the_borders`). Pixels from a neighbour are a seam
  (`Data.joined`: no anchors on its edge), never `filled` (`filled_m`, the map's filter); a filling layer that filled
  no pixel is not credited (FABDEM too). Help texts name no countries, they point to the table.
- The cache's tile encoding: `sources.layer._write_tile`'s profile (LERC_ZSTD within `MAX_Z_ERROR`), `old_encoding`
  (what counts as written before it), `Download.fetch` (a provider's one-band float file is written through it after
  its sha256 check; integer files, NASADEM and the water mask, as they came), `tools/sk_tiles.py` `_write_averaged`
  (lossless ZSTD, `AVERAGED_PROFILE`: `test_slovak_tiles_averaged` pins the mirror's profile and bits; such a tile is
  one float band that is not LERC, which `old_encoding` calls old by its header alone: only `GridTiles`' `rewrites`
  False keeps the mirror's copies from `reencode` and `cache migrate`,
  `tests/test_cache_encoding.py::test_an_averaged_zstd_tile_is_never_encoded_again`), `tests/tile_values.py`
  (`TILE_EPS` 0.0051: the bound plus float32 rounding) and the regression baselines go together: a change of the bound
  or the codec changes every `lines.csv` checksum (55 of 64 entries moved on 2026-11-05, 4 one-line spots at 0-1 cm of
  clearance lost and 4 added), needs `old_encoding` to tell the tiles of before, and `cache migrate` on every data
  folder (the VPS: `cache_migrate` of `deploy/vps.sh update`, `tests/test_deploy.py::test_cache_migrate_step`,
  docs/deployment.md section 13). The layout, the paths and the 11 columns of `files` stay (older code reads LERC and
  inserts rows by position); `filling.SEA_FILL_EPS` (5 mm around a source's constant sea fill) is the one rule as
  tight as the bound (`tests/test_cache_encoding.py`).

## Pitfalls

- **Half-pixel grid alignment**: data whose pixel centres lie on whole metres (geoportal sheets: `xllcenter`) must get
  `grid_origin=0.5`, otherwise the mosaic interpolates it bilinearly half a pixel off and smooths cliff edges (R2 at 1
  m lost 40% of its lines). Comparing rasters from two services needs the same care. A neighbour's data at a border
  lie off the grid by construction (another CRS, another origin): at the grid's own resolution they are reprojected by
  nearest, never bilinearly (`filling.NEIGHBOUR_SAME_RESOLUTION`), and coarser data never by nearest (a staircase of
  false walls).
- **Telling surveys apart**: two services resample differently, so compare their difference with the difference of the
  candidate source files (difference of differences); a residual around 0 shows which survey each serves (used for
  KRON86 vs EVRF2007).
- **WCS services differ**: axis labels (`x/y` vs `E/N`), multipart responses (coverage part may be `image/*` or
  `application/*`), formats (some GeoTIFFs are whole metres; ASCII grids keep centimetres), server limits (~2000×2000
  px), interpolation support. Malta's WCS answers 404 to a request that reaches beyond the coverage, which once made
  the whole north coast of Gozo "missing": `WcsTiles` clips such a request to the DescribeCoverage extent. A `missing`
  tile at the edge of a country's data deserves suspicion. Seen since: the Environment Agency answers HTTP 500 (not 404)
  for a tile wholly outside its coverage; Mecklenburg-Vorpommern's MapServer answers 400 for one wholly outside and
  stores the sea and the extent outside the Land as 0 in both layers, Hesse's rasdaman answers 404 beyond its extent
  and stores 0 outside the Land in its DOM (-9999 in its DGM); Saxony-Anhalt's ArcGIS WCS writes its multipart headers
  with bare LF, refuses more than about 2000 px (400) and answers 0 without nodata outside the Land; South Tyrol's
  GeoServer refuses SCALESIZE, clips partial requests, takes `GEOTIFF:COMPRESSION=DEFLATE`, and blends the pixels next
  to its nodata edge with 0 (`edge_px=3`).
- **Patched names** (`_get`, `_sleep` of `sources.net`, `country_code` of `sources.choose`, `DOWNLOAD_THREADS` of
  `sources.layer`): call and patch them through their module (`net._get(...)`,
  `monkeypatch.setattr(src.net, "_get", ...)`), never import them by name; the package does not re-export them, so a
  patch of `sources` itself fails (`tests/test_catalogue.py` checks both). Some tests load `progress` again, so a test
  listens to the bars through the object the code holds (`search.progress`), never through a fresh import of the
  module.
- **Usage limits of the providers** (the audit of 2026-11-01: docs/roadmap.md, "Audit of the services"): hosts whose
  provider publishes a request rate are in `sources.net.HOST_RATES_OF` (requests per minute, about 10 % below the
  terms, each quoting them: swisstopo's data.geo.admin.ch 36 and WMTS 1080, the Environment Agency 180);
  `net.rate_turn(url)` spreads their requests over every process of the machine (a pyrate-limiter SQLite bucket,
  `findaline-rates.sqlite` in the system's temporary folder, polled with `timeout=0`: the library's blocking wait
  admits early; `osm._nominatim_turn` polls the same way). Every request path to a rated host takes a turn:
  `net._get`, STAC pages, `WcsReader.coverage_extent`, orthophoto tiles, the probe, `check_source`; a 429 or 503 backs
  off longer. `net.HOST_SLOTS_OF` caps connections per host (BEV 2: its AGB §10 calls automated mass downloading
  misuse; Funet 4: it limits addresses to 10 connections; INSPIRE, provincial and LAiV servers 4; geoportal.gov.pl's
  WFS sheet indexes 4: no published limits). A GDAL window read holds one slot and counts as one request. A new
  numeric limit found in a provider's terms goes into one of these dicts, with the quote, URL and date;
  `tests/test_rates.py` (conftest empties `HOST_RATES_OF` for the other tests).
- **Keys of data services** stay in the worker: a key goes into a request only when it is made (`key_params`), never
  into the catalogue, a printed text, a run's files or a map's data; every message passes `cache.clean_error` (masks
  key values, also URL-encoded) and the public site's texts `jobs.Redact`. An orthophoto that needs a key cannot be
  used yet: the browser asks the WMS of a map's data.js itself (Denmark's, Finland's). Datafordeler (Denmark): WCS
  1.0.0 only, `apikey` in the query, 401 for every request without a valid key (GetCapabilities too), a key works only
  from the IPs on its IT-system's allowlist and lasts 2 years.
- **GDAL remote reads can fail silently**: a range answer cut short becomes nodata or zeros without an exception (GDAL
  3.12's /vsicurl/ keeps the short answer for the process, libtiff caches the unreadable strile offset as 0, GTiff
  fills the block as sparse; rasterio only logs it). Read remote rasters inside `_gdal_http()` (`GDAL_NUM_THREADS=1`:
  errors of GDAL's own threads go unseen; retry codes with 0), check `net.gdal_errors()` around open and read, and
  `rasterio.cache.invalidate(url)` before trying again (`sources/readers/windows.py`, the probe's `_window`,
  `tools/check_source.py`); `tests/http_server.py` `fault` cuts answers short.
- **GDAL's warper on Windows** (GDAL up to 3.13; rasterio 1.5.2's wheels hold 3.12.2): `reproject(num_threads > 1)` is
  GDAL's `ChunkAndWarpMulti`, one joinable thread per chunk, and `CPLCreateJoinableThread`'s race (OSGeo/gdal#15452,
  fixed on GDAL's master by #15451, in no 3.12 or 3.13 release) frees a thread's record twice when the thread ends
  before `CreateThread` returned: exit code 3221226356 (0xC0000374), no traceback, a worker's log stops mid-tile. A
  strip its source covers in part (a mosaic of two degree tiles: the water mask, FABDEM) is split into 40-55 chunks,
  many without a source pixel, so the country layer's workers died at about 1.4 of 1000 such stitches (32 times in 81
  550 tiles of rounds 4 and 5, never at a stitch of one tile; a stress of 12 processes 41 times in 15 minutes, 0 with
  `mosaic.WARP_KWARGS`, 2026-11-08). Never pass `num_threads > 1` to `reproject`: `num_threads=1` with the warp option
  `NUM_THREADS=N` keeps the kernel's N threads (`mosaic.WARP_KWARGS`). `faulthandler` sees a heap corruption: it
  prints `Windows fatal exception: code 0xc0000374` and each thread's Python stack, never a C stack (checked with a
  double free of ucrtbase's and in the warper's stress); the country workers have it on (`country._worker_env`).
- **Heights above the ellipsoid** (ArcticDEM: the sea at 27-32 m in Greenland and Svalbard): a layer of them names its
  geoid (`FileBlocks(geoid=EGM2008)`) and caches its blocks above it, so the water filling, the sea at 0 m and the
  heights shown stay as for every other model. Never PROJ for the geoid: pyproj's transformation to EGM2008 heights
  gives N = 0 without a word when its grid is missing (PROJ_NETWORK off); `sources/vertical.py` reads the grid itself,
  checked against GeographicLib's GeoidEval (`tests/test_vertical.py`, within 1 cm).
- **Water/gap filling order**: sea zone first, then gaps on land outside it, then the shore faces from the surface
  model (`fill_shore_from_surface`: before 2026-11-04 the lower 5-15 m of Malta's sea cliffs, which its DTM lacks and
  its DSM has, went to 0 m, 26 500 pixels above 20 m along its coasts, a wall straight down in the 3D view), then
  water. Gap offsets are matched on gentle slopes only and limited to ±3 m from the overall offset; no anchors at gap
  seams. Cliffs can be vertical: high land next to water is normal.
- **A grid smaller than its outline**: `grid.area_shape` may be more than the grid (a country layer's tile carries the
  whole country's outline, `search.tile_grid`; a border tile's footprint, cube-v2's `reach_shape`, reaches beyond its
  box), so whatever picks data tiles, index tiles or cells under an outline also stays within the grid's box
  (`tilings.cells_under`'s `within`, `cached.index_tiles`). 04e91b0 (2026-11-08) dropped the box: until 2026-11-11
  every interior tile of Slovakia listed the 12 715 files of each layer of the whole country and the mosaic opened
  each (about 33 s a tile, 31 of the lead profile's 32 stitching hours), the counts unchanged (files outside the
  window are skipped). A test of such code takes grids inside, across and outside the outline
  (`tests/test_units.py::test_tilings_within_the_grids_box`).
- **Updates** (`update.py`): a dataset that can change needs a TTL (`cache.TTL_DAYS`, `Layer.ttl_days` from `/live` in
  the folder) and a way back from its path to its tile (`Layer.tile_of`), otherwise `cache update` skips it.
  `osm.THEME_TAGS` defines a theme for Overpass, and for the extracts when it is in `osmextract.EXTRACT_THEMES`: a new
  theme is one entry there (and one in `EXTRACT_THEMES` to take it from the extracts: `osm update` then takes every
  extract's features again).

## Verifying changes

- Cache update code: a scratch `--data-dir`, `checked_at` aged by hand in its `index.sqlite`, then `cache status` /
  `cache update` (see the regression doc).
- New download code: test on an empty cache (`tests/regression.py --cache <temp>/cache`, or a scratch `--data-dir`),
  so the real cache is not touched.
- New data source or download path: `uv run tools/check_source.py <id> --lat .. --lon .. --data-dir <scratch>` (the
  shift search, the share of valid pixels at 1 and 5 m, a few tiles against the source files, alignment; see the
  regression doc, "Data checks for a new source"), and `findaline.py sources check <id>` for the probe (all sources:
  after changing a layer kind or `sources/net.py`).
